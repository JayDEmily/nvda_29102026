# 09.1_DMPV3_PROTOCOL_WORKING

All repo paths in the numbered control stack are repo-root-relative.

## Status

This working overlay contains admitted alpha-stage expansion of `docs/09_DMPV3_PROTOCOL_CORE.md` for packet occurrence identity, execution-context identity, lineage field meaning, validation scope, summary and extension limits, replay carriage, and implementation truth.

The core canonical envelope and field set remain binding. This overlay does not make the DMP envelope the owner of source evidence, domain objects, decisions, state, policy, capital, execution, exit, close, or review meaning.

## 9.1 Packet occurrence identity and source context

A DMPV3 packet is one emitted carriage occurrence. Its outer `id` identifies that packet occurrence within the scope declared by `source`.

The combination of `source` and `id` must be unique for each distinct DMP packet occurrence. A transport retry or exact re-delivery of the same preserved packet may retain the same pair. A semantically new packet, a transformed packet, or a newly emitted replay result must receive a new packet occurrence identity.

`packet.id` must not be used as the authoritative identity of an upstream source artefact, observation, domain object, decision, position, execution, policy, state transition, receipt, or preserved original packet unless the owning contract expressly defines that same object as the packet occurrence itself. Convenience equality does not merge those meanings.

`packet.source` identifies the bounded context in which the DMP packet occurrence was created. It does not mean the external data provider merely because provider material appears in the payload. The external provider and source-evidence identities remain governed by `docs/03.1_DOMAIN_MODEL_WORKING.md` and must be preserved in their own typed payload or lineage fields.

`producer` identifies the concrete module instance that creates the DMP data structure. `source` and `producer` may be related but are not interchangeable: one producer may emit on behalf of a broader packet source context, and a source context may be served by more than one lawful producer only where uniqueness coordination is explicit.

A canonical packet builder may construct packet-occurrence metadata only within its admitted packet-carriage authority. It must not manufacture an upstream source, domain, decision, state, policy, receipt, trace, run, or scenario identity merely to satisfy field shape.

## 9.2 Execution-context identity

`traceid`, `runid`, and `scenarioid` are distinct execution-context identities.

- `traceid` correlates packet occurrences that participate in one admitted trace context.
- `runid` identifies the governed execution or replay run under which the packet was emitted.
- `scenarioid` identifies the admitted scenario, specimen, or operating context where the applicable contract requires one.

Those values must be supplied by the boundary that owns the corresponding execution context. A packet builder propagates them; it does not acquire authority to invent them.

Where a governed packet role requires one of those values and the execution-context owner has not supplied it, the binding packet emission must fail closed or remain explicitly non-admitted. Deriving `traceid` or `runid` from `packet.id`, or silently substituting a generic value such as `live`, is not lawful governed identity.

A bounded test fixture may use deterministic fixture identities only when the fixture contract labels them as test-owned and no output is represented as live, historical, source-issued, or otherwise authoritative.

Trace correlation and immediate parentage remain separate meanings. `traceid` names the wider trace context; `lineage.parentids` names immediate causal upstream packet occurrences. Neither substitutes for the other.

## 9.3 Lineage field semantics

The DMP lineage block declares how the packet occurrence relates to authoritative evidence, packet occurrences, domain outputs, decisions, and replay context. It is not an untyped bag of identifiers.

The current lineage fields have these bounded meanings:

- `parentids`: identities of immediate causal upstream DMP packet occurrences from which this packet occurrence was produced;
- `dependencyids`: identities of other explicitly consumed packets, state snapshots, decisions, receipts, configuration records, or governed dependencies that are material but are not represented as immediate causal parents;
- `derivedfrom`: the authoritative upstream domain-output identity from which the payload's principal domain meaning was derived, where one such identity exists under the applicable contract;
- `replayref`: identity of the preserved original packet, decision, receipt, or replay evidence that the current replay occurrence is evaluating or reproducing;
- `sourceartifactids`: repository-capture or source-artefact identities for authoritative captured evidence used by the packet payload;
- `inputfingerprint`: the admitted semantic fingerprint of the exact material inputs under the applicable contract, not an unexplained hash of arbitrary serialised bytes;
- `reviewtraceid`: identity of the governed review trace when review material is consumed or produced;
- `replaytraceid`: identity of the governed replay trace for the current replay activity;
- `decisiontraceid`: identity of the governed decision trace when the packet carries or depends on a material decision.

An identifier may appear in more than one lineage field only when it truthfully has each declared meaning; duplication must not be used to conceal an unknown relationship.

Where more than one principal upstream domain-output identity must be represented, the applicable contract must provide a typed plural carrier or lawfully evolve the lineage schema. Multiple identities must not be concatenated into the singular `derivedfrom` field.

Every packet role must eventually declare its minimum mandatory, optional, conditionally required, and not-applicable lineage subset. That role-by-role completion belongs to the top-down architecture walk in Gate B. Until then, a caller must not infer that an empty default lineage block proves that no material dependency existed.

## 9.4 Temporal mapping subset

DMPV3 inherits the repo-wide source temporal standard admitted in `docs/04.1_TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE_WORKING.md`.
This section defines only the DMPV3 temporal mapping subset.
It does not restate or replace the full source temporal law.

`packet.time` is packet emission time.
`packet.time` is not source observation time.

Where packet carriage, payload content, lineage, persistence, or replay/proof surfaces need source observation time, that source observation time must be preserved separately through the admitted source temporal fields where relevant.
That preservation uses `source_time_raw`, `source_time_kind`, `source_timezone_raw`, `source_time_utc`, `source_date`, and `time_granularity` rather than overloading `packet.time`.

If the source truth is date-level only, the carried surface must preserve `source_date` and must not invent a finer source timestamp.
If the source truth is lawful local-clock time with a declared timezone or offset, the carried surface must preserve `source_time_raw`, `source_timezone_raw`, and `source_time_utc` consistently.
If a workbook-derived specimen blends fields with different source dates, packeted or packet-adjacent surfaces must preserve field-level provenance rather than fabricate one false same-moment source observation timestamp.

DMPV3 must not invent source-time precision the source did not provide.

## 9.5 Validation scope and emission law

The validation block records the checks actually performed for the admitted packet role and emission boundary. Its status is not a general statement that the source data, domain conclusion, trading proposition, downstream action, or whole system is correct.

`valid` means every check required for that packet role at that boundary passed and no applicable violation remains. A lawful optional absence or declared not-applicable field is not a violation merely because it is absent.

`degraded` may be used only where the applicable packet-role contract expressly permits non-binding or limited carriage despite identified limitations. Every limitation must be named in `checks`, `violations`, or another admitted typed field. A degraded packet must not be consumed as binding authority where the failed or missing fact is mandatory for that authority.

A packet that fails a mandatory structural, identity, lineage, semantic, or authority check must be rejected before governed emission, stand down, or be carried through a separately admitted diagnostic/rejection contract. It must not be emitted as an apparently usable live packet merely because a payload model can be serialised.

The default presence of `schema_validation=true`, an empty violations list, or successful Pydantic construction does not by itself prove the packet `valid`. The producer must know which checks are required for the exact packet role and record their actual outcomes.

## 9.6 Summary law

The summary is a deterministic, human-facing projection of facts already present in the governed packet contract or its recorded validation result.

It must not:

- create or override source, domain, policy, decision, state, capital, execution, exit, close, or review authority;
- conceal a validation limitation or contradict `data`, `lineage`, or `validation`;
- become a machine decision input where the typed packet contract provides the authoritative field;
- introduce source-time precision, certainty, or maturity that the packet does not contain.

Free-form explanatory wording may be included for inspection, but any material flag used by downstream logic must live in an admitted typed contract rather than only in `summary`.

## 9.7 Extension law

`dmpextensions` remains bounded compatibility metadata. An extension must have an admitted name, owner, meaning, type, versioning rule, and consumer rule before it becomes governed input.

An extension must not:

- carry a mandatory canonical field that should have been present in the outer envelope or typed payload;
- hide source, domain, decision, policy, state, execution, exit, close, review, or lineage authority;
- weaken fail-closed behaviour;
- create unversioned behavioural branching;
- overwrite or contradict canonical fields or repo-owned `nvda` compatibility leaves;
- be interpreted by payload-shape guess or nearest-known meaning.

Unknown extensions may be preserved or ignored under the receiving contract. They must not be reinterpreted as known authority merely to continue processing.

## 9.8 Replay carriage law

Reading a preserved original packet returns that original packet with its original `source`, `id`, `time`, payload, lineage, validation, summary, and extensions unchanged.

A newly emitted replay request, replay result, comparison packet, or reconstructed domain result is a new packet occurrence. It receives a new packet `id` and emission `time`, and it identifies the preserved original or authoritative replay evidence through `replayref` and the governed replay context through `replaytraceid`.

Deterministic re-execution is assessed against the admitted semantic result and material-version fingerprint under `docs/04.1_TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE_WORKING.md`. It is not represented as byte-identical reproduction of an original envelope when current emission metadata has been regenerated.

A transport duplicate of an already preserved packet is not a new replay result. It may retain the original `source` and `id` only when the packet is otherwise the same preserved occurrence and the receiving boundary treats the pair as duplicate identity.

## 9.9 Prepared context and review packet subset

`prepared_context_packet` is the canonical DMPV3 packet role for bounded prepared-runtime-context handoff material. It carries `producer.module_id=prepared_ingress`, `producer.grammar_role=prepared_runtime_context`, `producer.behaviour_class=preparation_output`, and `producer.stage_name=runtime_context_preparation`.

`review_explanation_packet` is the canonical DMPV3 packet role for deterministic review/explanation material. It carries `producer.module_id=review_explanation`, `producer.grammar_role=review_explanation`, and `producer.behaviour_class=review_only_output`.

Both packet roles preserve lineage and proof material. Neither role authorises live trading, capital deployment, risk weighting, order placement, or source-time precision invention.

## 9.10 Current alpha implementation truth

`src/nvda_desk/contracts/packets.py` and `src/nvda_desk/packets/dmp_packets.py` are the current canonical implementation allocation for packet contracts and builders. The compatibility wrappers under `src/nvda_desk/schemas/` remain non-owning aliases.

Those paths are implementation evidence, not the source of protocol meaning. This normative pair owns the protocol law, `docs/07` owns runtime semantic ownership, and each typed payload contract owns its domain fields.

The current builder defaults that derive `traceid` and `runid` from `packet_id`, default `scenarioid` to `live`, default module-instance identity, manufacture wall-clock emission time, and mark the result `degraded` are current alpha implementation truth. They do not satisfy governed identity, replay, or validation law merely because the resulting model validates. Their correction belongs to Gate D after role-specific contracts and Gate C proof are accepted.

Current default-empty lineage fields, inferred schema-identification extensions, generated human summaries, and unrestricted validation-status strings similarly require role-specific reconciliation. Their presence must not be reported as proof of complete lineage, deterministic replay, or end-to-end protocol compliance.

## External-source lineage for this overlay

- CNCF CloudEvents specification and primer, reviewed 2026-07-17: support the distinction between event occurrence, source context and concrete producer; uniqueness of `source` plus `id`; independent schema/type evolution; and explicit extension semantics.
- W3C Trace Context Recommendation, reviewed 2026-07-17: supports separating the wider trace identity from immediate parent-operation identity and rejecting invalid context rather than silently normalising it.

The project adopts those identity-separation and explicit-context principles only. It does not adopt distributed deployment, HTTP trace-header formats, message brokers, CloudEvents processing models, or vendor infrastructure as project requirements.
