## 1.1 Authority-scope clarification

The authority hierarchy in `docs/01_NORMATIVE_CORE.md` governs the meaning of the system and the precedence of repository doctrine.

It does not displace direct user authority over the project or the execution-control hierarchy that determines which files may be read, changed, proved, or promoted during a bounded work programme.

For execution and mutation authority, use this order:

1. direct user instruction and explicit approval;
2. repo-root `AGENTS.md`;
3. the lawfully routed active bounded pack and its ExecPlan;
4. the numbered normative documents that govern the active semantic question;
5. tests;
6. source, configuration, schemas, fixtures, migrations, and data;
7. ledgers, receipts, reports, and retained historical evidence.

For system meaning after a decision has been admitted into the repository, use the semantic hierarchy in `docs/01_NORMATIVE_CORE.md`:

1. `docs/01_NORMATIVE_CORE.md` together with an expressly admitted numbered working overlay;
2. the other numbered normative documents inside their owned boundaries;
3. repository configuration and schemas where doctrine delegates exact values or shapes;
4. tests;
5. source code and fixtures;
6. historical or ephemeral material.

A direct user decision that changes system meaning must be incorporated into the appropriate numbered normative document before tests or code are changed to implement that meaning.

Tests, source code, generated artefacts, ledgers, receipts, audit reports, and historical plans may reveal inconsistency. They do not create or silently alter normative meaning.

## 1.2 External-evidence adoption rule

Current authoritative external material may inform and challenge project architecture.

External material does not remain a floating override above the normative corpus. A proposition becomes project authority only after the project records:

1. the source and review date;
2. the architectural problem being decided;
3. the project-specific interpretation;
4. the alternatives considered or rejected where material;
5. the accepted decision and its implications;
6. the exact numbered normative proposition affected;
7. whether the decision is current, provisional, reserved, or superseded.

Where authoritative sources disagree or address a different operating context, the project must record why one interpretation fits this deterministic alpha system. Vendor-name voting is prohibited.

## 1.3 Controlled amendment and supersession rule

The numbered core/working-overlay model is the controlled change mechanism for normative doctrine.

A core document is the frozen baseline for its owned boundary.

A working overlay may expand, clarify, or supersede an exact proposition only when the change is expressly admitted by current user authority and the active bounded pack.

Each admitted change must state:

1. status and effective date;
2. context and problem;
3. decision;
4. affected core or earlier-overlay proposition;
5. whether the earlier proposition remains active, is narrowed, or is superseded;
6. rationale and material implications;
7. external-source lineage where external evidence was used;
8. unresolved reservations or Unknowns;
9. the tests and implementation surfaces that must later conform.

Accepted historical decisions must not be silently rewritten. If an accepted decision changes, a later numbered overlay must supersede it explicitly and preserve the earlier decision for lineage.

A contradiction is not resolved by adding another statement while leaving both statements apparently active.

## 1.4 Alpha truthfulness rule

This repository is an alpha architecture under construction.

A placeholder, thin seam, provisional allocation, reservation, or in-memory implementation must be labelled honestly and must not be described as production-ready, durable, replay-complete, operationally recovered, or end-to-end validated without direct proof.

The normative corpus must not invent production retention periods, recovery objectives, deployment constraints, capacity targets, availability targets, or paper-trading evidence before those requirements and evidence exist.

Alpha status does not relax identifier ownership, lineage, boundary, deterministic behaviour, modularity, or fail-closed authority requirements. Those structural properties must be established before incomplete behaviour is expanded.

## 1.5 Semantic reconciliation rule

Normative coherence and acceptance require full semantic reading by the responsible LLM worker.

Inventory, search, grep, scripts, generated tables, hashes, ASTs, schemas, embeddings, summaries, and counts may assist navigation or mechanical validation. They must not decide or certify semantic equivalence, contradiction, absence, authority, intent preservation, architectural adequacy, modularity, future-proofing, or final acceptance.

Each numbered normative core and working companion must be read in full and interpreted in the context of all materially related normative propositions before it is accepted, amended, or frozen.
