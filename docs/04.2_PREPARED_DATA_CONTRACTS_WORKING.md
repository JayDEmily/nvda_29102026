## 04.2.N03 Prepared runtime substrate common envelope

### 04.2.N03.1 Boundary decision

Runtime preparation must emit a stable prepared runtime substrate whose common envelope is structurally separate from its typed payload variant. The common envelope carries identity, lineage, version, temporal, validation and reproducibility facts that apply to the prepared material as a whole. The payload carries only an admitted, explicitly tagged contract variant for the prepared fact family.

Neither the common envelope nor the payload may be represented at a governed cross-boundary seam solely as `Mapping[str, Any]`, `dict[str, Any]`, an untagged mapping, or an arbitrary extension bag. A mapping may exist only as a bounded serialisation view of an already validated typed contract; it is not the authoritative contract itself.

This package governs the prepared boundary. Package `N-05` separately governs which common-envelope facts are referenced or carried into the common stage context and which facts belong only in stage-specific carriers. `N-05` may not weaken the prepared-envelope requirements defined here.

### 04.2.N03.2 Mandatory common-envelope facts

Every accepted prepared substrate must preserve:

1. one repository-owned prepared-substrate identity;
2. one or more owner-issued source or capture identities for every contributing admitted input;
3. the admitted input-family and source-contract identity for each contributing input;
4. the exact input schema version for each contributing input;
5. the preparation-policy version and every material derivation version used;
6. per-source effective, observed and captured temporal facts at their original precision, plus the runtime alignment time and alignment-policy version where alignment occurred;
7. an input fingerprint covering the admitted source material or immutable source references;
8. a prepared semantic fingerprint covering the typed payload and material envelope facts under a declared fingerprint policy version;
9. source artefact references sufficient to retrieve or identify the admitted evidence under the owning retention policy;
10. validation outcome, completeness state, explicit limitations, failed mandatory transformations and missing optional enrichments;
11. ordered parent-prepared identities when the substrate was derived from earlier prepared material; and
12. the explicit payload-variant discriminator and payload schema version.

The prepared-substrate identity, capture/source references, input schema version, preparation-policy version, fingerprint facts, payload discriminator and validation state are mandatory. A source-issued identifier may be absent only where the admitted source contract states that the source does not provide one; the repository-owned capture identity remains mandatory and must not be fabricated from an unrelated packet, trace, run, timestamp, filename or local sequence.

### 04.2.N03.3 Typed payload variants

Each prepared payload variant must:

- have one governed semantic name and version;
- forbid undeclared fields at its authoritative validation boundary;
- express bounded missing, unavailable and not-applicable states explicitly where the source contract permits them;
- contain prepared facts and derivations only, without candidate, risk, capital, placement, execution, inventory, close or review authority; and
- remain substitutable behind the preparation boundary without requiring unrelated serial, risk or downstream modules to understand provider-specific shapes.

A multi-source prepared substrate must retain per-source identity, version and temporal facts. It must not collapse different source facts into one anonymous provenance object or infer a single source time from whichever value is non-empty.

### 04.2.N03.4 Identity and fingerprint law

The prepared-substrate identity identifies the governed prepared object. It does not replace source identity, capture identity, packet identity, trace identity, run identity or scenario identity.

The input fingerprint identifies the admitted input evidence or immutable source references. The prepared semantic fingerprint identifies the deterministic prepared result. Both must declare the fingerprint policy or canonicalisation version used. A prepared result must not claim reproducibility when either fingerprint is absent, contradictory or calculated over an ungoverned mapping whose field semantics are unknown.

Where exact source bytes are not retained by the owning source contract, the envelope must identify the immutable external artefact or governed capture record used. A hash alone is evidence of equality, not evidence that the material can be retrieved for authoritative replay.

### 04.2.N03.5 Validation, failure and degradation

Preparation must fail closed and withhold an authoritative prepared substrate when any mandatory identity, source/capture reference, schema version, policy version, payload discriminator, required temporal fact, fingerprint, typed payload validation or mandatory transformation is missing, invalid or contradictory.

A permitted optional enrichment gap may produce an explicitly degraded prepared substrate only when:

- the payload contract admits the absence;
- the common envelope names the missing fact and its consequence;
- no mandatory input or transformation has failed; and
- downstream consumers are able to distinguish the degraded state from complete prepared truth.

An empty mapping, default object, current wall-clock value, inferred provider, locally invented identifier or successful model construction must not convert missing authority into accepted prepared truth.

### 04.2.N03.6 Ownership and longitudinal use

Source adapters own provider translation and capture identity. Runtime preparation owns the prepared-substrate identity, typed transformation, preparation and derivation versions, prepared fingerprint and validation/completeness result. The source domain continues to own source truth; preparation does not become its writer.

Serial cognition, Parallel Risk Lane, capital and downstream consumers may read prepared facts through admitted typed contracts and governed lineage references. They must not mutate the prepared substrate, widen its payload, replace missing facts, or reinterpret an envelope field as trading authority.

### 04.2.N03.7 Current alpha consequence

Current `PreparedRuntimeSnapshot`, `PreparedRuntimeDataset` and `PreparedContextPayload` surfaces demonstrate useful bounded preparation, copy separation, temporal alignment and explicit optional-enrichment state. They do not yet conform to this authority because identity is optional, the payload and derived fields are generic dictionaries, source/capture identity and schema/policy/derivation versions are incomplete, and no mandatory prepared semantic fingerprint exists on the substrate contract.

This is an implementation consequence only. No production source, runtime configuration, schema, fixture or migration change is authorised by this addendum.

## 04.2.N04 Canonical merge conflict and source precedence

### 04.2.N04.1 No global source order

There is no repository-wide precedence order such as market before options before provenance, provider A before provider B, latest before earlier, non-empty before empty, file order, insertion order or dictionary update order. Canonicalisation must resolve each semantic field under an explicit field-resolution rule owned by the applicable source or preparation contract.

A field-resolution rule must name the semantic field, admitted candidate sources, source-family roles, required type/unit/temporal alignment, equality tolerance where applicable, conflict class, authorised selection or substitution rule, required lineage, and failure/degradation outcome. A generic merge helper may execute those rules but may not invent them.

### 04.2.N04.2 Candidate preservation and equality

Before canonical selection, preparation must retain the identity, value, unit, temporal applicability, source/capture reference and validation state of every admitted candidate value for the semantic field.

Candidates that are semantically equal under the declared type, unit, temporal and tolerance policy may coalesce into one canonical value. Coalescence must preserve all contributing candidate lineage and must record that equality, rather than implying that one source silently overwrote another.

### 04.2.N04.3 Authorised selection and substitution

A canonical value may be selected from unequal candidates only when the field-resolution rule expressly authorises one of the following for that semantic field:

1. one named source role owns the field and other candidates are corroborating only;
2. one named source role is an authorised fallback when the owning value is absent or invalid under a stated condition;
3. a versioned source-quality, effective-time or confidence rule is semantically valid for that field and all compared candidates satisfy its comparability requirements; or
4. an authorised operational correction supersedes a named earlier fact under the applicable domain-specific law, including the calendar law in `docs/03.2`.

The receipt must retain all candidates, the selected value, selected source/capture identity, rule and policy version, any fallback or supersession reason, and any rejected candidate with its rejection reason.

Freshness, confidence or non-null status is not inherently authoritative. It may decide a field only when the field-resolution rule names it and defines how incomparable precision, stale intervals, source quality and contradictory values are handled.

### 04.2.N04.4 Unsafe conflict and bounded degradation

If unequal candidates exist for a mandatory single-valued semantic field and no field-resolution rule lawfully resolves them, canonicalisation must fail closed for that field and withhold an authoritative prepared substrate.

A conflict may produce an explicitly degraded or multi-valued prepared fact only when the typed payload contract expressly permits that state and downstream consumers cannot mistake it for one authoritative value. Candidate retention without selection is not a silent default and may not cross into a consumer that requires a single resolved fact.

A missing candidate is not a conflict. A malformed, temporally inapplicable or contract-inadmissible candidate must be recorded and excluded under the rule; it must not be treated as an equal candidate or silently converted to a default.

### 04.2.N04.5 Field-level lineage and replay

Each canonical field must carry or reference a deterministic resolution receipt containing:

- semantic field name and output contract version;
- ordered candidate records with source/capture identities and fingerprints;
- normalisation operations, units and temporal alignment applied before comparison;
- equality/tolerance result;
- selected, coalesced, unresolved or degraded outcome;
- field-resolution rule and policy version; and
- conflict, fallback, supersession and rejection reasons.

Authoritative replay must be able to reapply the same rule to the same candidate evidence and reproduce both the selected value and the resolution receipt. A final canonical value without its field-level receipt is insufficient where more than one candidate source was admitted.

### 04.2.N04.6 Ownership, substitution and current alpha consequence

Source adapters own translation into source-family candidates. Runtime preparation owns rule execution and the canonical-field resolution receipt. The source/domain contract owns the meaning and precedence rule. Downstream cognition, risk and capital may consume the selected typed fact and its governed lineage but may not re-resolve the source conflict.

Adding or replacing a provider is lawful only by adding or changing its source adapter and the affected field-resolution rules. It must not require unrelated downstream modules to learn a provider-specific order.

Current `_combine_raw_sections` logic applies deterministic but implicit market/options/provenance selection and special-case spot handling without field-level candidate receipts or unsafe-conflict rejection. That is a non-conforming implementation consequence only; no implementation change is authorised here.
